# CHAPTER SEVENTEEN — The Whale Road

The Hollows came at them out of the deep water on the fifth day, and there were eleven of them, and they were not hunting.

That was the first wrongness. Hollows hunted — unfinished things went through the world looking for their missing feeling, and every report, every manual, every two-hundred-year nightmare of the third district agreed on that one fact. These did not hunt. They *herded*. Eleven grey shapes under the Glass — the sea flat as a slate over them, their shapes readable the way a shape is readable under ice — moving with the Wrong Way at exactly the Wrong Way's speed, all day, holding formation like a honor guard, or a net.

"They've been with us since the academy's cliff," Yuki said, at the rail, slate out. "I thought they were current. They're not current. They're *station-keeping*."

"And they're all facing the same way," Ryudo said, from the bow, where he had spent the day with his sword across his knees, reading the water the way he read travelers. "Not at us. *Past* us. Something behind us is worse than we are, and these things are running from it in formation."

The humming had been louder since dawn. That was the second wrongness: the bilge-hum, the sister's glad patient tune, had climbed all morning — up through the deck-boards, into the rigging, into the crew's teeth — and Ren, palms open at the stern, finally named what the sound was doing, because it had stopped being ambient and started being *communicative*:

She wasn't humming along with the sea. She was humming *at* it. At the eleven shapes under the Glass. Calling them, the way you call something you outrank.

And the sea answered.

It came up without breaking the surface — the Glass did not ripple; the Glass *opened*, the way the sky had opened, flat, hinged, total — and what rose through the opening made the whole crew take one step back from the rail in perfect unison, because the manuals had a name for what the deep water sometimes made, and no one had ever seen one and written it down:

A Hollow that commanded Hollows.

It was whale-sized. It was whale-shaped, roughly, the way the market Hollow had been man-shaped — *roughly*, the proportions half a step off, the flukes too many-jointed, the head bearing, where a whale's blowhole should be, a door. A door the size of a cart-horse, grey, shut, old — and around it, as it surfaced, the eleven herders rose too, and lined up, and *bowed*, in the water, all together, and the sound that came out of the great door was not a song and not a knock.

It was a voice. A whole drowned town's voice, speaking in unison.

"*Kept,*" the Hollow-king said. The word came out of the door like tide out of a cave. "You carry a *kept* thing, small boat. We can smell it. Two hundred years we have swum this road, and kept things are the only cargo we do not touch — and the only cargo we *follow*."

"We know," Ren said, palms open, the umbrella's shape, the only shape that had ever once worked on things from the deep water. "She's my sister."

"*Yours,*" said the Hollow-king, and the eleven herders bowed again, lower, and the great door tilted — the way a head tilts — and the unison voice did something no Hollow's voice had ever done in the history of the hall's records, something Yuki would write three entries about before nightfall and the saga would spend sixty chapters unwinding:

It went *quiet with feeling.*

"The kitchen-tune," the Hollow-king said. "Two hundred years, small boat, we have heard the tithes of the whaling towns leak down through the Glass — the grief of the hunt, the last songs, the empty water — and we are made of those leavings, all of us out here, the whole pod, the whole road — and in two hundred years we have never once heard a tune that was *going somewhere*. Everything on this road is unfinished. That is what the road is. And your bilge-water hums like a thing that knows what it is waiting for." The door opened, a hand's width — and through the gap came warmth, whale-warmth, the warmth of a town's whole memory of the last whale, and the unison voice said, quietly, with the terrible gentleness of the very large:

"We want to ask it something. May we?"

Ren looked at the rail. At the crew. Yuki, slate dead in her hands, had stopped writing; Kaito had his compass out and the needle was spinning; Ryudo's hand was on his sword and not on it, hovering, the swordsman's whole two-hundred-year discipline failing him in the face of a thing that *asked permission*.

"She's not here to be questioned," Ren said. "She's here to be found. But — she's my sister, and she's glad, and I think—" and he made the choice the whole road turned on, the choice the lampwright's bank had taught him, the choice that would take him up every rung to come: he went below, and came back up with the bilge.

He carried the humming up in the ship's soundings-jar — a gallon of collected rain, grey, flat, *loud* — and set it on the rail between himself and the Hollow-king, and the jar hummed, and the great door came down close, and the sea held still from horizon to horizon.

And the Hollow-king — made of a whaling town's whole unfinished grief, two hundred years of the hunt's leavings, the last songs and the empty water — *listened* to a six-year-old's kitchen-tune, all the way through, for the first time in its existence.

What it did at the end of the tune was: it finished a word.

One word. The unison voice, a drowned town speaking together, said — softly, out of the ajar door, like a man completing a sentence two centuries late — "*…stay.*"

The word went out over the Glass. The eleven herders rose from their bow. And the Hollow-king drew back from the rail — the whole whale-sized cathedral of it — and the door shut, gently, and the voice said, from deeper, from the opening it had made in the sea:

"The road is yours, small boat. We will not touch it again. And take this, for the bilge-water's brother: your compass is ours — the needle was cut from our door's hinge by a thief with a lit grin, two years ago, and we let him keep it, because a needle that points at *wanting* is the only kind we have ever blessed." The sea began, very slowly, to close over the opening, flat, hinged, total. "But hear the last of us, and hear it carefully, because we are the road's oldest thing and we do not say it twice: you are being *followed*. Not by us. Not by the pod. By something that walks the sea floor — something even the pod does not approach — something that has been walking since the night of the thirteenth bell, in your wake, at *your* speed, patient as tide." A pause. The water at the edges of the opening going flat. "It does not hum, small boat. It does not knock. It does not ask. It is the only thing on this road that is not unfinished — and that is why we fear it. Finished things, on this sea, are *rare*. And rare things get *collected*."

The sea closed. The Glass went flat. The eleven herders sank away in their formation, and the Wrong Way ran on alone into the dusk, and the crew stood at the rails for a long time, nobody writing, nobody laughing, the compass needle in Kaito's fist spinning, spinning, spinning—

—and stopping. Straight down. Pointing, through the hull, through the flat dead sea, at the thing that walked below them in the dark, at their speed, at their course, patient as tide.

And humming — no. Ren, palms open, listening down through the deck-boards, corrected himself with a coldness that started in his spine and never left the saga again:

Not humming. *Footsteps.* Something below them, walking the sea floor, following the ship's wake like a man following a friend home.

And it was *whistling* the kitchen-tune.
