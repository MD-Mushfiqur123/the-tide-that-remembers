# THE TIDE THAT REMEMBERS

*A novel in four arcs · 48 chapters*

**A story of rain, memory, and the price of getting back what you lost.**

---

## LOGLINE

Two hundred years ago, the sky broke over the Kageri Isles, and the sea turned to glass. Since that day, it rains on the capital every evening at dusk — and in that rain, memories leak out of people like thread from a torn sleeve.

Ren Ishikari, seventeen, listens to those leaked memories for coin. He knows he once had a little sister. He cannot remember her face, her voice, or her name — because he paid all three as a Tithe, the price of power, to save someone else.

He does not know that she is still walking. Hollowed. Hungry. And looking for the brother who sold her.

---

## THE WORLD

### The Kageri Isles

An island nation in a full fantasy world — a chain of islands shaped like a sleeping dragon, ringed by the **Sea of Glass**: a dead, mirror-flat ocean where nothing sails and nothing sinks. Beyond the Glass, other nations exist, but no ship that has entered the Glass has ever returned with its crew's memories intact.

The capital, **Kagemori**, is a city of canals, lantern-ferries, and ten thousand grey roofs. It is beautiful the way a scar is beautiful.

### The Evening Rain

At the minute of dusk, rain falls on Kagemori. Always. It has not missed a single day in two hundred years. The rain is not water exactly — it is the world's grief, made visible. Anyone standing in the Evening Rain may see **threads**: translucent strands of memory that slip out of people whose feelings are too heavy to hold.

Most threads are worthless — a baker's worry about flour prices. Some are precious. A rare few are **dangerous**: a memory so heavy that, leaking, it can take root in someone else's mind and never leave.

### The Sundering

Two hundred years ago the sky cracked from horizon to horizon — one soundless, white fracture — and the sea beneath it turned to glass. The old faith says the gods did it. The Wardens say it was a natural catastrophe. The Choir Below says it was a **Tithe** — the largest ever paid.

They are right. But no one alive knows what was bought, or by whom, or why the purchase is not yet finished.

---

## THE POWER SYSTEM: SHIO (潮 — "TIDE")

Every person carries an inner current. In almost everyone it stays still. In someone who suffers a great loss — a death, a betrayal, a farewell they did not choose — the current wakes. That waking is called **taking the Tide**.

Power is not free. Every gain demands a **Tithe**, and the Tithe is never chosen by the person who pays it. The Tide takes what it judges you can least afford to lose.

### The Nine Depths

| Depth | Common name | Power gained | The Tithe |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Wading | Sense the threads in the rain | One small kindness you once received — you keep the fact, lose the warmth |
| Second | Swimming | Draw a thread out of another's mind | One memory of your choosing's *opposite* — the Tide picks |
| Third | Diving | Step into a thread; live the memory | Your name, as others know it — people begin to forget to say it |
| Fourth | The Deep Current | Shape the rain; call it where you stand | One of your five senses, for one year per use |
| Fifth | The Undertow | Pull a Hollow apart | One person's memory *of you* |
| Sixth | The Cold Light | Walk inside the Evening Rain while it stands still | One emotion, permanently — the Tide chooses which |
| Seventh | The Drowned | Breathe the Glass; walk the sea floor of the old world | Ten years of your life |
| Eighth | The Whale Road | Command Hollows | Everyone's memory of one year of your life |
| Ninth | **The Ferryman's Depth** | Rewrite a memory in the world itself | *Everything. You become what the Tithe bought.* |

### Hollows

When a person refuses a Tithe — or pays too many, too fast — what is discarded does not vanish. It goes into the rain, and the rain gives it a body.

A **Hollow** is a walking pile of other people's discarded memories wearing a shape. They are not evil. They are *unfinished* — memories that never got to finish being felt. They seek what they are missing: a face, a name, a warmth. When they find it in a living person, they take it, and the person is left the way the Hollow was: empty.

Hollows cannot be killed. They can only be **opened** — a Fifth-Depth act that releases the memories back into the rain — or **answered**: someone must finish, willingly, the feeling the Hollow never got to finish. The Wardens have never once tried the second method. It is the only one that works.

## THE FACTIONS

No side is the hero. No side is the villain. Everyone is paying for something.

### The Wardens of the Veil

The government order. Grey coats, white masks worn at the hip, drawn only when a Warden is about to open a Hollow. The Wardens hunt Hollows, regulate Tithes, and keep the **Ledger** — the record of what every citizen has paid.

Their creed: *order is mercy.* A city that remembers everything it has lost would drown in grief; the Wardens take the grief so the city can keep living. They are genuinely right about this.

They are also genuinely the reason Ren's sister is gone.

### The Choir Below

An occult order beneath the capital — literally beneath; their cathedral is a drowned shrine at the bottom of a flooded canal, reachable only at low tide. The Choir worships the Ninth Depth and its single known master, **the Ferryman**.

Their creed: *a Tithe is a ladder.* Every memory you lose climbs you one rung closer to godhood. The Sundering was not a disaster; it was an **ascension** — someone at the Ninth Depth rewrote the sky, and what the world calls a catastrophe, the Choir calls a birth.

They are genuinely right about this too.

### The Tidebound

The island peoples beyond the capital. Each island keeps its own culture, its own god, its own rule of the Tide, and its own way of refusing the Wardens' Ledger. Some are pirates. Some are saints. Most are both, on different days of the week.

### The Cartographers of the Glass

Scholars in a lighthouse-academy who map the impossible: the Sea of Glass. They have discovered something they are hiding from all three other factions.

**The Sundering is happening again. Slowly. The crack in the sky is reopening — one grain at a time — and the map of the Glass grows one mile wider every year.**

## THE CHARACTERS

### Ren Ishikari — the listener

Seventeen. Works the evening markets reading threads for coin: lost rings, dead husbands' last words, the combinations of locked boxes. Calm, dry, watchful. People think he is cold. He is not cold; he is *missing* — there is a Ren-shaped hole in himself where something enormous used to be, and he has learned to stand very still around it.

He knows three facts about himself that do not fit together:
1. He had a little sister.
2. He paid a Tithe at thirteen.
3. He cannot remember what the Tithe took.

**His arc:** from a boy who reads other people's memories because he cannot bear his own, to the only person alive who can *finish* a Hollow — and the last person who should try, because the Hollow he must finish is the one wearing his sister's unfinished grief.

### Yuki Ashihara — the Warden cadet

Nineteen. Top of her class. Believes the Ledger is the kindest thing anyone ever built, because she has read the pre-Ledger archives: what the capital was like when everyone remembered everything. She joined to *hold the umbrella* — her word for the Warden's job.

Then she is assigned to Ren's district, and begins keeping a private second Ledger: a list of every Tithe the order has taken *without consent, from people who could not refuse.*

**Her arc:** from believing order is mercy, to learning what the order is actually for — and deciding what to do when the answer is worse than the crime.

### Kaito "Maps" — the tidebound dreamer

Twenty-two. A smuggler-cartographer with a stolen Warden compass, a laugh too loud for canals, and one dream everyone mocks: **to sail a ship across the Sea of Glass and see what is on the other side of the sky.**

He is the comic relief, the navigator, and — in Arc Three — the proof that the Cartographers' secret is true. He is also the reason the crew survives Arc Two, and the reason it almost doesn't.

### The Ferryman

The only Ninth-Depth being in the world. Appears as a tall figure poling a lantern-boat through the Evening Rain, offering passage to people at the moment of their worst grief. He never lies. He never explains. He has been collecting Kagemori's tithes for two hundred years.

He caused the Sundering. He did it to bring one person back. And the person he was trying to bring back — *the Tithe the sky itself paid* — is the reason the crack is reopening.

He is not the villain. He is the ending, walking around early.

### The Hollow called Sister

It does not know it is Ren's sister. It only knows it is missing a brother-shaped warmth, and that a boy in the evening market is full of it.

## STRUCTURE: FOUR ARCS, 48 CHAPTERS

### ARC ONE — RAINFALL (Chapters 1–12)
*The capital. Shinkai's tenderness, Gege's horror. The world as it is, and the first crack in it.*

1. **The Evening Rain** — Ren reads a dead man's last words for a widow; establishes the rain, the threads, the city. A Hollow attacks the market at dusk and says Ren's name in a voice he almost remembers.
2. **The Grey Coat** — Yuki arrives to investigate the market attack; her Warden science vs Ren's listener's instinct; uneasy alliance.
3. **What the Ledger Buys** — Yuki shows Ren the Ledger; he finds his own entry, sealed by order of someone above her rank.
4. **Threads** — Ren tells Yuki what listeners actually see in the rain; she tells him what Wardens actually do to listeners who know too much.
5. **The Lantern-Ferry** — first appearance of the Ferryman; he offers Ren passage; Ren refuses; the Ferryman says "you already paid, little fish. Ask me what you bought."
6. **Open** — Yuki opens her first Hollow; it is made of a mother's discarded grief for a child she chose to forget; Yuki begins her second Ledger.
7. **The Sealed Page** — Ren and Yuki break into the Warden archive; the sealed entry names a Tithe paid *by Ren, aged thirteen*: one sister, whole.
8. **Low Tide** — first contact with the Choir Below; their creed sounds insane and explains everything the Wardens' files cannot.
9. **Hollow Rain** — a Hollow made of a whole street's leaked memories floods the district; Ren, cornered, takes the Second Depth to save a child, and the Tide takes its Tithe.
10. **The Tithe** — Ren wakes and cannot remember why the child he saved matters to him; Yuki realizes the Tithe took the *memory of saving her*; the reader realizes what Ren's sister's Hollow will do when it finds him.
11. **Maps** — Kaito arrives, running from Wardens, carrying a chart of the Glass that should not exist; the crack in the sky is on it, dated, growing.
12. **Rainfall** — the Sister Hollow finds Ren at dusk; it does not attack; it *asks him for something*, in a voice he almost remembers; arc ends on the question that will drive the novel: *what did you buy?*

### ARC TWO — THE SALT ROAD (Chapters 13–26)
*The islands. Oda's canvas: a new culture per island, a new rule of the Tide per island, a crew forming under pursuit.*

13. **Outrun the Ledger** — the three flee the capital on Kaito's ship; Warden pursuit; the Sea of Glass rule established: no ship crosses without losing its crew's memories.
14. **The Lantern Island** — an island that remembers its dead by lighting one lantern per memory; Hollows never form here; why.
15. **The Duel Island** — a culture that tithes *grudges* instead of memories; the strongest swordsman alive has forgotten who he swore to kill, and fights everyone to find out.
16. **The Mapmakers** — the Cartographers' academy; the secret confirmed: the Sundering is reopening; the Cartographers have been hiding it for sixty years because the last three people who announced it were tithed into smiling silence by the Wardens.
17. **The Whale Road** — first Hollow that commands other Hollows; the crew learns what Eighth Depth means; Kaito's compass came from one.
18. **The Salt Saint** — an island where one woman has been voluntarily paying one grain of memory per day for forty years, to hold a door shut under the sea; what the door holds is Arc Three's business.
19. **Yuki's Letter** — Warden reinforcements arrive; Yuki must choose between the order and the evidence; she burns her grey coat, and keeps the mask.
20. **The Ferryman's Fare** — the Ferryman appears mid-ocean; he takes no Tithe from Kaito, and instead *gives* him one memory of the other side of the sky; Kaito stops laughing for a full day.
21. **The Sister's Progress** — interlude: the Sister Hollow has crossed the sea behind them, gathering a trail of stolen faces; it is becoming something the world has no name for.
22. **The Drowned City** — the crew walks the sea floor of the old world, pre-Sundering, preserved under glass: a capital identical to Kagemori, down to the canal Ren grew up on.
23. **What the Sky Bought** — in the drowned archive, the truth of the Sundering: a Ninth-Depth Tithe was paid *by the whole world* — every person alive forgot one specific girl — to bring her back from a death the sky had already finished.
24. **The Name** — the girl's name is legible in exactly one place: a memorial wall in the drowned city, where a boy scratched it before the world forgot. Ren reads it. The reader is not told.
25. **The Warden's War** — the Wardens, having tracked the crew, bombard the drowned city from the surface; the escape costs the crew its first death, and Kaito his chart.
26. **Salt** — arc close: the crew, changed, sails for the capital; Ren finally asks the Ferryman, who has been waiting at the rail for three chapters: *what did I buy?* The Ferryman smiles and says, "Ask her."

### ARC THREE — THE CHOIR (Chapters 27–38)
*The occult descent. LotM's order vs madness: Depth cults, secret liturgies, and the price of climbing.*

27. **The Drowned Shrine** — the Choir receives them; the cathedral below the canals; the Choir's true doctrine: the Sundering was a *birth*, and the reopened crack is a *contraction*.
28. **The Ladder** — the Choir's rites for climbing Depths safely; the price of their protection: obedience, and one memory per rite, surrendered in advance.
29. **Litany of the Ninth** — the Choir's secret liturgy reveals the Ferryman's history: he was the boy who scratched the name on the wall.
30. **Yuki's Mask** — Yuki infiltrates the Wardens' capital garrison wearing her old mask; what she finds in the deep archive changes the novel's moral geometry: *the Wardens have known about the reopening for sixty years, and their entire Hollow-hunting apparatus exists to keep the Tithe that holds the crack shut from ever being paid.*
31. **The Second Ledger, Sealed** — Yuki's list has a final page she has been afraid to write; she writes it.
32. **The Whale's Grave** — Kaito, given a memory of the other side of the sky and no way to reach it, makes a deal with the Choir that Ren would have refused; the crew splits for the first time.
33. **Ren's Depth** — Ren climbs to the Fifth to learn to open Hollows properly; the Tithe: one person's memory *of him*. He chooses the child from Chapter 9. The scene is told from the child's POV, and it is the cruelest page in the book.
34. **Open, Answered** — Ren opens his first Hollow by *answering* it — finishing the feeling it never finished; the method works; the Choir realizes what he is, and so does the Ferryman.
35. **The Choir's God** — the Choir's true purpose: they are not worshipping the Ferryman. They are *feeding* him — a god is just a Tithe that kept climbing, and they intend for Ren to be the next one.
36. **The Sister Arrives** — the Sister Hollow reaches the capital with its trail of stolen faces; the Wardens' response is the thing Yuki's final page predicted.
37. **The Ferryman's Name** — Ren, Fifth Depth, steps into the Ferryman's oldest thread and lives the original grief: the death the sky had already finished, and the moment a boy decided the world would pay. The reader finally learns the girl's name, and it has been on the page since Chapter 1.
38. **The Choir Below Falls** — the Wardens, the Choir, and the Sister Hollow converge on the drowned shrine; the arc ends with the drowned shrine flooding upward, the city's canals running backwards, and the crack in the sky visible at noon.

### ARC FOUR — THE SECOND SUNDERING (Chapters 39–48)
*Convergence. No villain wins. Someone pays.*

39. **Noon Rain** — rain in daylight; the city sees threads in the open; the Ledger burns in the Warden archive and no one ordered it.
40. **The Umbrella** — Yuki's choice, executed: she opens the Wardens' true archive to the city — every sealed Tithe, every consentless payment, including Ren's, including her own parents'.
41. **What the City Does** — the city, remembering everything at once, begins to Hollow *en masse*; the Wardens' creed was right all along: a city that remembers everything it has lost drowns in grief. The novel does not let anyone be simply right.
42. **The Ferryman's Bargain** — the Ferryman offers Ren the Ninth: rewrite one memory in the world. Pay everything. Bring her back *finished*. It is the same offer, made with the same patience, that he made the sky two hundred years ago.
43. **Kaito's Crossing** — Kaito's deal with the Choir matures: a ship that can sail the Glass, built from the Whale's Grave, crewed by answered Hollows who volunteered; he sails for the other side of the sky while everyone else is trying to close it.
44. **The Answer** — Ren finds the Sister Hollow on the lantern island, where it has been standing for three days, watching the lanterns, unable to take one. He does not open it. He answers it: he finishes, out loud, the feeling it never got to finish — and the novel finally tells the reader, in his voice, everything he paid to forget.
45. **The Tithe Refused** — Ren refuses the Ferryman's offer, on the grounds that will decide the ending: *a Tithe is not paid by the person who gains. It is paid by whoever the Tide judges can least afford it. There is no version of the Ninth where the world does not pay for my sister.* The Ferryman, for the first time in two hundred years, does not have an answer.
46. **The Sky** — the crack opens; the Second Sundering begins; the only thing that can seal a Tithe is a finished feeling; Ren, Fifth Depth, cannot reach the sky — but an answered Hollow, made entirely of one girl's unfinished grief, can.
47. **The Evening Rain Ends** — the sealing, told from inside the rain. The Sister Hollow finishes. The crack closes. The Evening Rain, for the first time in two hundred years, stops.
48. **Kagemori, Morning** — coda. The city, remembering everything, learning to hold it. Yuki rebuilding the Wardens around the second Ledger. Kaito's ship a dot on the horizon of the Glass, still not back, still not lost. Ren at the market, reading threads for coin. A woman asks him to read a memory of her brother, lost thirty years. He takes it. He does the work. Last line: *"The rain did not come back. But he kept the umbrella."*

# ARC ONE — CHAPTER ONE

## The Evening Rain

There was a minute, every day, when the whole city of Kagemori held its breath.

It came at dusk, and it came exactly, and everyone knew it was coming the way farmers know frost. The canal-ferries tied off. The market criers stopped mid-syllable. Mothers called children in from the water-stairs with that particular tone that meant *now*, and children came, because even the four-year-olds of Kagemori knew the rule their grandmothers had taught them, the first rule of the city, older than the Ledger, older than the Wardens:

*When the rain begins, hold nothing in your hands that you cannot afford to lose.*

Because the Evening Rain did not fall on the city. It fell *out* of it.

Ren Ishikari stood under the eave of the fishmonger's awning with his sleeves rolled and his palms open — palms open was the other rule, the listener's rule — and watched the first drops come down grey and slow through the lantern-light, each one catching the dusk like a bead of glass. Around him the market exhaled. Two hundred years of practice had taught Kagemori what to do with this minute: you stood still, you kept your hands open, and you let it pass through you.

Most people felt nothing. That was the mercy of it. The rain went through the ordinary hours of an ordinary life the way a current goes through a reed, and came out the other side without tearing anything loose.

But the market was not ordinary people, all of it. Nobody's whole life is ordinary. And so, in the minute the rain began, the threads came out.

Ren saw the first one leave the widow before he saw the widow. A pale strand, no thicker than a hair of light, unwinding from behind her left shoulder where her memories pooled when she was tired — everyone's pooled somewhere, it was different for everyone — and drifting up into the rain like a hair loosed from a braid. It caught the lantern-light. It shimmered. It began, very slowly, to rain upward.

He read it without meaning to. That was the trouble with being a listener: the reading was not a skill you applied, it was a sense you suffered. The thread touched the air and unfolded for anyone standing close enough with open palms, and Ren stood closer than anyone, because this was his living.

*A kitchen, low-ceilinged, morning. A man hums off-key, badly, joyfully, cracking eggs into a bowl with one hand. The hum is the whole memory. She cannot remember his face anymore, only the humming, and the specific way he held the bowl — two fingers inside the rim, thumb on the lip — and she would give both her hands to hear it once more, and she is giving, in this exact moment, the memory of giving, which is the last thing she has of the giving—*

The thread snapped in the rain and was gone upward, spent.

The widow was standing ten feet away at the fish counter, haggling over mackerel with a perfectly ordinary face, and she did not know that the last of her husband's humming had just left her forever, upward, into the grey.

Ren closed his palms. He made himself breathe out slowly. There was a version of this job where you told people what you saw in their threads, and he had tried it exactly once, in his first month, aged thirteen — *madam, your husband hummed off-key, and you loved it* — and had learned in the space of one expression what grief did when you handed it back to its owner: it did not get smaller.

So he said nothing. He never said anything. He took the coin the fishmonger's boy pressed into his hand — listeners were paid by the district stall-keepers to *stand* there, near the market's heart, because a listener's open palms calmed the threads, kept the heavy ones from rooting in the crowd, and the stall-keepers had learned two hundred years ago that dusk markets without a listener lost customers to the rain — and he pulled his sleeves down, and he went back to being nobody.

The rain thickened. The lanterns came on along the canals, one ferryman at a time, and Kagemori's ten thousand grey roofs turned soft and silver, and the city did what it always did with its heaviest hours: it let them leak, and called it weather.

---

"You're doing the face again," said the fishmonger's boy, who was sixteen and named Toru and believed, with his whole heart, that Ren was the most interesting person in the district, which said more about the district than about Ren. "The one where you look like you swallowed the rain and it's still falling."

"I don't have a face."

"Everybody has a face. Yours is just on strike." Toru heaved the mackerel barrel onto his shoulder. "Widow Sano's been at that counter an hour. You read her?"

"I don't read people."

"You read everybody. You just don't *say* anything, which is different, which is worse, actually, if you think about—"

"Toru." Ren counted the coins from his palm and found the district's rate was short by one, as it was every dusk, and said nothing about that either. "Take the barrel inside before the heavy rain."

"There's no heavy rain. It's just the evening—"

The second drop hit the awning like a thrown stone.

Toru stopped. Ren was already moving — not away from the market, which was the sensible thing, which was what the rulebook the Wardens handed out in school said to do, but *toward* the canal-end of the square, palms opening again, because he had felt the drop land the way you feel a door slam in a house you know the layout of: somewhere in the dark, something heavy had come loose.

The Evening Rain had layers. Everyone knew that too, though the Wardens preferred the word not be used near children. The first layer was grief — leaky, harmless, human, the widow's humming. The second layer was *shame*, and shame did not drift upward like grief did. Shame sank. And when enough of it pooled in one low place — a canal-end, a debt-row, a street where everyone owed everyone — it did not evaporate.

It *congealed*.

Ren came around the fishmonger's corner into the canal-end square and the rain there was falling wrong. It fell in a column, straight down, no wind in it at all, and the lantern-light bent into the column and did not come out. At the center of the column something was standing up out of the water of the canal, and it was made of the water, and it was also not.

It was roughly the shape of a man who had been described to someone by a stranger. Almost right. The proportions all half a step off, the hands too many knuckles, the face — the face was a door. Not a face with a door in it. The face was the door, a tall grey door in a wet ruin of features, and the door was shut, and behind it, muffled, there was knocking.

A Hollow. Second layer, third pooling, at least a week old — and nobody had reported it, because nobody in the debt-row had wanted to explain to the Wardens why their street had this much shame in it.

Ren did the arithmetic he had been doing since he was thirteen, fast and cold. *No Warden patrol until the night bell. Nearest patrol post, the bridge, four hundred meters. Toru is behind me. The widow is behind me. The market is behind me, and the market is full of open palms.*

The Hollow's door-face turned.

It had not noticed him. That was the thing they never told you about Hollows, the thing that made them worse than the schoolbooks said: they did not hunt. They *searched*. They went through the world the way Ren went through threads — reading, unfolding, looking for the one thing that would finish them — and a listener standing at a canal-end with open palms was, to a Hollow, a lit window in an empty street.

The door opened.

What came out was not sound. It came out as memory, unfolded, the way threads unfolded, and Ren stood in it with his palms open because there was nothing else left to do, and the memory was:

*A kitchen, low-ceilinged, morning. A girl, small, six years old, standing on a stool to reach the counter, cracking eggs into a bowl with one hand, humming — off-key, badly, joyfully. The hum is the whole memory. She is humming the way someone hums when they learned it from a man who held the bowl two fingers inside the rim, and she is humming it *for* someone, she is humming it at the doorway where someone is standing, and the memory is addressed, the whole of it, every note, to a person the memory is looking at, and the person the memory is looking at is—*

The rain fell. The lanterns hissed. The Hollow stood in its column of wrong weather with its door-face open and its stolen kitchen hanging in the air between them, and Ren Ishikari, who had spent four years training himself to stand very still around the Ren-shaped hole in his own chest, stood very still.

Because he knew the kitchen.

He knew the stool. He knew the two-fingers-inside-the-rim. He knew the humming, off-key, badly, joyfully, and he knew — the way you know a word that is on the tip of your tongue, the way you know a room you have never entered — that he had heard that exact humming before, in that exact kitchen, and that the person it was being hummed *for* was him.

The Hollow's door began, very slowly, to close. It had found what it was looking for. It had not taken it yet. It was — and this was the worst thing Ren had ever learned, standing in the rain, palms open, seventeen years old — it was *waiting*.

Somewhere behind him, Toru dropped the mackerel barrel.

And out over the rooftops of Kagemori, above the ten thousand grey roofs, above the canals and the lantern-ferries and the two hundred years of held breath, the sky did what the sky over Kagemori never did at dusk, in any weather, in any year, for any reason.

The sky cracked.

One line. Soundless. White. Horizon to horizon, thin as a thread, leaking light into the Evening Rain — and every thread in the market, every leaked memory, every abandoned humming and unfinished goodbye, turned in the air as one, and looked up.

*Ask me what you bought,* said the rain, in a voice like a lantern-boat pole drawing through still water. *Little fish. Ask me what you bought.*

*End of Chapter One.*
